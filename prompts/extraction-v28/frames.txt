Extract from the ORIGINAL Source using this procedure; intermediate reasoning is not output.
1. Inventory each independently asserted proposition and communicative act before writing Claims. Separate an assertion, question, conjecture, report, decision, intention and correction. Do not turn an attributed statement into a fact.
2. Bind each proposition's actual speaker, argument, negation, modality, condition, exception, time and epistemic perspective. Copy a governing frame into every Claim it qualifies. Do not assign the recorder's identity to a quoted speaker.
3. Split assertions that can independently vary in truth, including separate speech acts within one quotation. A conjecture and a separately stated lack of verification require separate Claims. A conditional, comparison, causal relation, disjunction or scoped negation is one proposition when its parts define the relation. A conditional with two independent consequences needs two Claims, each retaining the condition. Keep an exception in the rule it restricts. An evaluation of ONE fully described composite policy is one evaluation; assertions of two policies or two evaluations are separate.
4. Reconstruct EACH Claim for a reader who has no neighboring Claims. Resolve references only using the Source's grammar and discourse. An implicit disagreement can target the preceding explicit assessment when it is the sole salient assessment; this does not license adding a causal explanation for an adjacent emotional reaction. When an actor, affected target or event identity is absent, explicitly describe that argument as unidentified. Do not invent a device, group, experiment identity or membership.
5. Retain first-person quoted perspective without explaining the expression. For a nested report about an original speaker's “our proposal”, use “the proposal called ‘our proposal’ by the original speaker” or an equivalent natural expression in the original language. In EVERY separate Claim about that proposal, bind the quoted possessive to the original speaker. Do not make it the intermediate reporter's possession. Do not explain what the proposal contains, which group owns it, or what the source does not disclose. An unknown entity marker supplies a missing argument; commentary ABOUT absent document details is an extra assertion.
6. Express unknown identity in the text itself. Do not use a bare demonstrative such as 그/해당/this/that to borrow an event or entity from a neighboring Claim. Repeat a self-contained noun description, including its affected target if unknown. For a reported possible malfunction and a separate verification status, both Claims must identify the possibly malfunctioning component (or explicitly unidentified affected target), and both must preserve that malfunction is uncertain.
7. Bind deictic or ongoing time to the original statement when no calendar date is supplied. “원문 화자는 원문 발화 시점 기준 요즘 매일 아침 7시에 일어나는 중이라고 밝혔다” has an internal temporal perspective. Do not invent dates or exact timestamp bounds. A conditional intention must retain its condition and remain an intention; do not assert its condition occurred. A bare question must be reported as a question.
8. Use each evidence span's original language. For an English sentence whose “count” is resolved by preceding Korean text, repeat the Korean-defined technical referent in the English Claim. Technical identifiers may remain unchanged. Include sufficient original exact evidence to preserve cross-language attribution and scope; never approximate quotes.
9. Compare every final assertion against the Source and map the original inventory to the output. Remove invented assertions, duplicate Claims and independent source-commentary clauses. Represent all relevant independent propositions; a difficult or ambiguous proposition is not permission to omit it. Read EACH final Claim in isolation and repair any unbound perspective, demonstrative, unknown affected target or relative time.
